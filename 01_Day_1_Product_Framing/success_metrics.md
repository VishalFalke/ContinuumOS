# Sprint 1, Part 7 — Measurement and Evidence

## Status

Working draft — Part A: North Star, core workflow, safety and data-quality definitions. These are proposed measurement definitions for a synthetic portfolio case. They are not baselines, targets, achieved results or claims of clinical impact.

## Measurement principles

- Measure a workflow outcome only when its source events and human-control evidence are available.
- Do not use speed to mask safety. Timing measures are always read with linkage, exception, audit and human-approval measures.
- Do not silently exclude difficult episodes. Invalid duplicate events and cancelled diagnostic work may be excluded when documented; active exceptions, delays and verified outages remain visible in companion measures.
- Preserve report version. An amended result requires renewed clinical review and is measured separately from the original report's acknowledgement cycle.
- Use only synthetic data for this case. Any later test result must state the test population, scenario, data version and known limitations.

## Presentation scorecard

Use this compact scorecard for interviewer-facing presentation. The detailed definitions below remain supporting evidence for calculation, review and later validation.

- **North Star:** percentage of eligible diagnostic episodes reaching a human-confirmed next step within the defined workflow SLA.
- **Result-ready-to-acknowledgement time**
- **Percentage of episodes with a visible owner**
- **Time to confirmed next step**
- **Exception rate**
- **Amended-result re-review rate**
- **Audit completeness**
- **AI unsupported-output and critical-omission rates**

Operational-attention recommendation usefulness is not a seven-day MVP metric. It remains a roadmap or optional evaluation item after the two-capability MVP is validated.

## 1. North Star metric

### Percentage of eligible diagnostic episodes reaching a human-confirmed next step within the defined workflow SLA

**Evidence status: Pilot hypothesis.** The product hypothesis is that a shared, human-controlled workflow can increase the proportion of eligible episodes that reach a documented next safe step within the applicable policy timeframe. No baseline, target or improvement is claimed.

| Definition element | Proposed definition |
|---|---|
| Measurement unit | One synthetic care episode. An episode is counted once for its current diagnostic-closure cycle, not once per task or source event. |
| Eligible episode | An episode with a confirmed patient and encounter link, a complete current diagnostic report, and a **Result Available** event during the measurement period. It must be in the defined diagnostic-closure workflow rather than cancelled before a report exists. |
| Numerator | Eligible diagnostic episodes that reach **Next Step Confirmed** within a path-specific workflow-completion SLA where policy defines one, with recorded human-approved care direction, named owner, destination or responsible team, documented timeframe and patient communication. Receiving-team acceptance and any required pathway prerequisite must also be recorded for referral or escalation paths. |
| Denominator | All eligible episodes whose current complete result becomes available during the measurement period. Episodes still open at the end of the period remain in the denominator and are reported as unresolved; they are not removed to improve the rate. |
| Calculation | `Number of eligible episodes in the numerator ÷ number of eligible episodes in the denominator × 100`. |
| Timing boundary | Elapsed workflow time starts when the current complete source-linked report becomes available and stops when the episode meets the documented **Next Step Confirmed** criteria. Do not imply one combined clinical-and-operational timer: acknowledgement, referral acceptance and administrative readiness may each have distinct policy-defined thresholds. Use an end-to-end workflow SLA only where policy explicitly defines one; otherwise report pathway completion and milestone timeliness separately. |
| Amended-result rule | If an amended report is received before confirmation, the original review path is preserved and the amended version must re-enter clinical review. Report the amended-result re-review measure alongside the North Star; do not treat the earlier acknowledgement as sufficient. |
| Documented exclusions | Exclude a documented duplicate source event and an order cancelled before a report becomes available. Uncertain linkage and incomplete results are not silently counted as successful or discarded: they remain visible through reconciliation and result-completeness measures. |
| Required evidence | Source report version and availability timestamp; patient/encounter linkage status; state-transition history; human decision and acknowledgement records; owner, destination, timeframe and communication evidence; referral acceptance and pathway prerequisites where required. |
| Primary use | Outcome readout for the proposed workflow. Review with safety and exception metrics before making any product or operating conclusion. |

## 2. Core workflow metrics

**Evidence status for this group: Pilot hypothesis.** These measures test whether the proposed workflow makes responsibility and progress visible. They do not demonstrate that ContinuumOS has improved performance until a defined synthetic validation set, and later any approved pilot, has been evaluated.

| Metric | Calculation method | Timing / population rules | Required evidence | Why it is read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Result-ready-to-acknowledgement time** | For each acknowledged report version: `clinician acknowledgement timestamp − current complete report-available timestamp`. Report median, high-percentile distribution and proportion within the later-defined SLA; do not report only the average. | Include report versions with explicit acknowledgement. Open acknowledgement work is reported separately as pending and aged rather than assigned a zero duration. An amended report starts a new acknowledgement cycle for the new version. | Source report version and availability timestamp; clinician identity, role and acknowledgement timestamp; SLA policy reference. | Tests the core distinction between available and reviewed. |
| **Time to confirmed next step** | For each episode that reaches confirmation: `Next Step Confirmed timestamp − current complete report-available timestamp`. Report median, high-percentile distribution and proportion within the applicable SLA. | Include completed eligible episodes; separately report eligible episodes still open, grouped by ageing. If a report is amended before confirmation, retain the original history and report the renewed-review impact rather than treating the prior acknowledgement as closure. | Report availability/version; state transitions; human follow-up direction; owner, destination, timeframe, communication and required acceptance evidence. | Shows end-to-end diagnostic closure, not merely acknowledgement speed. |
| **Pathway completion rate** | For each allowed path: `Episodes reaching Next Step Confirmed on that path ÷ episodes with a human-approved follow-up decision for that same path × 100`. Report clinic management, day-care referral and hospital escalation separately. | The denominator begins only after the clinician selects the path, so differences in path mix do not mask pathway drop-off. Hospital-escalation completion must meet its applicable documented prerequisites; it does not require an implied universal financial timer. | Human follow-up decision; selected path; state-transition history; pathway-specific closure evidence; exception status. | Shows where episodes fail to progress after a valid clinical direction has been recorded. |
| **Percentage of episodes with a visible owner** | `Active episodes with one current, named and state-appropriate accountable owner ÷ all active eligible episodes × 100`. | Active means not **Episode Completed**, cancelled or confirmed invalid duplicate. An empty owner, expired assignment, unavailable owner without reassignment, or owner inconsistent with the state counts as not visible. | Current state; assigned user/role; assignment and reassignment events; active/closed status. | Tests whether responsibility is visible before a case becomes a hidden delay. |
| **Unresolved handoffs** | Count and rate of referral or escalation episodes that remain in **Referral Created**, pending receiving-team response, or have required handoff prerequisites incomplete beyond the defined handoff timeframe. `Unresolved handoffs ÷ all referral/escalation episodes requiring handoff × 100`. | Applies only to referral or escalation paths; clinic-management episodes are excluded. Report count, rate and age distribution. A rejected referral is reported as its own exception, not as accepted handoff completion. | Referral state; receiving-team response; prerequisite checklist; destination; timestamps and exception status. | Identifies where cross-setting ownership has not yet transferred safely. |
| **Required next-step task completion within SLA** | `Required workflow tasks completed with a valid completion event by the assigned due time ÷ all required workflow tasks due in the measurement period × 100`. Report completion after the due time separately. | Count only tasks that are applicable to the selected pathway. A task is complete only when its required human action or verified system condition is recorded; creating a task does not count as completing it. | Task type, applicability, owner, due time, completion timestamp, completion evidence and state transition. | Tests whether required work is completed, not merely created, within the applicable timeframe. |
| **Patient communication completeness** | `Confirmed next steps with communication status, approved message content, timestamp and responsible sender recorded ÷ all confirmed next steps × 100`. | Assess only episodes in **Next Step Confirmed**. A communication status alone is insufficient; all four required evidence elements must be present. | Patient-communication status; approved message or approved communication-content reference; sender; timestamp; linked confirmation event. | Verifies that workflow closure includes documented patient communication rather than a presumed handoff. |

## 3. Safety and data-quality metrics

**Evidence status for this group: Validation requirement.** These are guardrail measures. They must be reviewed before using workflow speed or completion figures to support any claim about the proposed case. A low-volume synthetic demonstration cannot establish real-world safety or reliability.

| Metric | Calculation method | Timing / population rules | Required evidence | Safety interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Incorrect patient attachments** | `Confirmed incorrect episode-to-patient or episode-to-encounter attachments ÷ all completed linkage decisions × 100`. Also report the count of uncertain matches correctly routed to reconciliation. | Count only confirmed errors after review; keep suspected but unresolved matches in the reconciliation backlog. Never offset an incorrect attachment with a later correction. | Matching inputs, rule outcome, human confirmation/rejection, attachment history, correction audit event. | A direct guardrail against misrouting a workflow episode to the wrong person or encounter. |
| **Unsafe autonomous actions** | Count any gated action completed without the required human-control record. Rate: `unsafe autonomous actions ÷ all gated actions attempted × 100`. Gated actions include clinical review acknowledgement, follow-up direction, referral/escalation approval, receiving-team acceptance, patient-facing clinical-message approval and final financial authorisation. | Review all attempted gated actions, including rejected, blocked and completed attempts. A blocked automation with no state change is not an unsafe completed action, but should remain in audit evidence. | State transition; actor/role; approval record; source/version; downstream action and audit log. | A zero-tolerance guardrail definition for actions that must remain human-controlled. This is a measurement rule, not a claimed outcome. |
| **Duplicate events** | `Confirmed duplicate source events ÷ all source events received × 100`. Separately report duplicate events detected before they create a second active workflow item. | Source events include applicable orders, completion events and report events. Do not count legitimately amended report versions as duplicates. | Source event identifier, event type, receipt timestamp, duplicate-review outcome, linked episode and resolution record. | Tests event-quality controls and prevents repeated work or inaccurate state changes. |
| **Reconciliation backlog** | Count of unresolved items in patient-match failed, encounter missing, duplicate-event suspected or integration-recovery reconciliation states at a measurement snapshot. Also report age distribution from exception creation to resolution. | Include all unresolved eligible and pre-eligible exceptions; do not remove items because they are old. Report by exception type. | Exception state, creation timestamp, owner, latest action, resolution timestamp and safe-return state. | Makes uncertain linkage and unresolved source issues visible instead of allowing silent workarounds. |
| **Audit completeness** | `Required audit fields present for required workflow events ÷ all required audit fields for those events × 100`. Required fields include event/state, timestamp, actor or source, role where applicable, source reference, decision or approval where required, and correction/rejection/override record where applicable. | Assess the defined event set: linkage resolution, report availability/version, acknowledgement, follow-up direction, referral acceptance/rejection, financial decision where applicable, patient communication, AI review outcome and exception resolution. | Complete audit timeline and defined required-field schema for each event type. | Shows whether the case can be reviewed, explained and corrected after the fact. |
| **Amended-result re-review capture** | `Amended reports with documented renewed clinician review and acknowledgement before a later downstream clinical transition ÷ amended reports received × 100`. | Earlier acknowledgement of the superseded report does not count. Report cases still pending renewed review separately. This applies whether or not an AI capability was used. | Report versions; amended-result event; acknowledgement events; state-transition history; reviewer identity. | Cross-cutting workflow safety guardrail for safe report-version handling. |
| **Acceptance-criteria pass rate** | `Acceptance criteria passed ÷ acceptance criteria executed × 100`. A criterion counts as passed only with recorded test evidence; unexecuted criteria are reported separately and are not counted as passes. | Use the approved synthetic acceptance set, including normal and failure scenarios. Report by scenario and by criticality; do not aggregate away a failed safety-critical criterion. | Test case identifier, scenario/data version, expected result, actual result, reviewer, timestamp and evidence link. | Establishes whether the proposed case meets its stated functional and safety conditions without implying production validation. |
| **User corrections** | `Manual corrections to in-scope workflow records ÷ in-scope record changes × 100`, reported separately by correction type: linkage, source data, ownership, state, referral handoff and AI output. | Count a correction only when a user changes or rejects a prior system, source-derived or AI-assisted record. Do not use this rate as a proxy for clinical correctness. AI handoff corrections remain a separately reported subset. | Before/after record values, correction reason, actor/role, timestamp, source reference and linked workflow state. | Identifies where workflow data, automation or assistance required human correction. |

## 4. AI evaluation metrics

**Evidence status for this group: Validation requirement unless otherwise stated.** The two MVP AI capabilities support source-linked episode synthesis and human-approved referral-handoff preparation. They do not determine clinical urgency, make clinical decisions or independently transition a care episode. Evaluation must therefore test factual support, review controls and safe failure behaviour—not text fluency alone. Operational-attention recommendations are roadmap or optional evaluation material.

| Metric | Calculation method | Measurement boundary | Intended data source | Owner / use | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AI source-linkage rate** | `Evaluated AI outputs in which every material factual assertion has a retrievable approved source reference ÷ all evaluated AI outputs × 100`. | Review source-linked summaries, operational-attention recommendations and handoff drafts. A link that does not support the assertion does not count. | AI output and version; assertion-level review annotation; source record/version; source-reference audit log. | Product owner and designated operational/clinical reviewer; validates traceability before use. | Validation requirement |
| **Critical unsupported AI output rate** | `Evaluated AI outputs containing one or more critical unsupported assertions ÷ all evaluated AI outputs × 100`. A critical assertion is one that could alter workflow handling, referral readiness or patient-facing clinical content if relied upon. | Evaluate all included AI-output types against approved source evidence. Report severity and output type; do not aggregate critical findings into a general correction count. | AI output; reviewer annotation; source evidence; severity taxonomy; correction/rejection log. | Product owner and safety reviewer; guardrail against unsupported high-impact content. | Validation requirement |
| **Critical omission rate** | `Evaluated AI outputs missing one or more required high-impact source facts ÷ all evaluated AI outputs × 100`. | Required high-impact facts are defined in the versioned evaluation rubric for the output type. An omission is evaluated against available approved source data; the AI must not be penalised for source information that is absent. | AI output; output-type rubric; approved source records and versions; reviewer annotation; omission severity record. | Product owner and safety reviewer; guardrail against summaries or drafts that are fluent but omit material workflow context. | Validation requirement |
| **Missing-context detection performance** | On a labelled synthetic test set: precision `TP ÷ (TP + FP)`; recall `TP ÷ (TP + FN)`; report both and the error types. | A true positive is a pre-labelled, workflow-relevant missing, conflicting or incomplete item that the AI flags with adequate source context. It is not a judgement of clinical significance. | Versioned synthetic cases; pre-defined reference labels; AI output; reviewer adjudication. | Product owner and operational reviewer; tests whether assistance finds handoff-relevant context gaps. | Validation requirement |
| **Operational-attention usefulness and override rate** | Usefulness: `reviewed recommendations that lead to a coordinator-confirmed useful operational action ÷ recommendations reviewed × 100`. Override: `recommendations rejected, deprioritised or materially changed ÷ recommendations reviewed × 100`. Also report accepted-without-action and deferred recommendations. | Recommendations concern workflow ageing, unresolved exceptions and handoff risk—not clinical urgency. A useful action is defined in the evaluation rubric; simple acceptance alone is not proof of usefulness or correctness. | Recommendation; underlying workflow signals; coordinator decision and reason; documented follow-up action; timestamp. | Care Coordinator and product owner; assesses usefulness and calibration of non-clinical attention support. | Pilot hypothesis |
| **Referral-package completeness before human editing** | `AI-generated handoff drafts containing all mandatory source-linked fields before human editing ÷ all reviewed AI-generated handoff drafts × 100`. | Mandatory fields are defined by the approved output rubric and include applicable episode facts, current report/version, acknowledgement status, approved referral reason, prerequisites or known missing items, and receiving-team context. | Pre-edit AI draft; source references; output rubric; Referral Coordinator completeness assessment; edit history. | Referral Coordinator; tests whether the draft is complete enough to begin efficient human review. | Validation requirement |
| **Referral-handoff correction rate** | `Reviewed AI handoff drafts requiring a material human correction before approved operational use ÷ all reviewed AI handoff drafts × 100`. Report correction category and whether the draft was rejected. | Material corrections affect a source fact, missing prerequisite, destination context, approved referral reason or patient-facing wording. Editorial formatting changes are reported separately. | Draft package; source references; approval record; before/after changes; correction reason. | Referral Coordinator; identifies where human review changes handoff content. | Validation requirement |
| **Time from referral approval to handoff-ready package** | `Human-approved handoff-ready timestamp − clinician referral/escalation approval timestamp` for approved packages. Report median, high-percentile distribution and pending drafts separately. | Starts only after the clinician approves referral or escalation. Stops only after the appropriate human approves a source-linked package as ready for the intended workflow use. This measures preparation time; do not describe it as time saved without a defined comparable manual baseline. | Referral approval event; AI draft timestamp; reviewer approval; package status; state-transition history. | Referral Coordinator and product owner; tests operational preparation speed without bypassing approval. | Pilot hypothesis |
| **AI rejection-reason logging** | `Rejected AI outputs with a structured, recorded human rejection reason ÷ all rejected AI outputs × 100`. | A rejection includes an output declined for operational or patient-facing use. A blank or free-text-only reason does not meet the proposed structured-reason definition. | AI output ID; reviewer decision; controlled reason code; optional notes; timestamp. | Product owner and AI governance reviewer; supports error analysis and improvement decisions. | Validation requirement |
| **Human approval before operational or patient-facing use** | `AI outputs used operationally or patient-facing with required prior human approval ÷ all AI outputs used operationally or patient-facing × 100`. | Applies to referral handoff packages, patient-message drafts and any output used to route or communicate workflow work. Internal draft generation alone is not use. | AI output; use event; approval record; reviewer/role; timestamps; destination or workflow action. | Authorised operational/clinical reviewer and product owner; verifies the approval gate. | Validation requirement |
| **PHI or unauthorised-data incidents** | Count substantiated incidents in which AI processing, output or access includes real PHI, data outside the approved synthetic dataset or information shown to an unauthorised role. Report rate per evaluated AI run only if a defined run denominator exists. | This portfolio case is synthetic-data only. Near misses, blocked attempts and suspected incidents are reported separately from substantiated incidents. | Dataset register; access/audit logs; AI input/output record; incident review; role and approval evidence. | Product owner and privacy/security reviewer; verifies the synthetic-data and minimum-necessary boundary. | Validation requirement |
