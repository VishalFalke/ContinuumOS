# Interview Presentation Outline

Prepare a concise six-to-eight-slide presentation:

1. Scenario, product intent, problem and why it matters
2. Current-state breakdown, operating users and unvalidated assumptions
3. Alternatives considered, selected approach, scope and opportunity costs
4. Workflow, decision rights, architecture and ecosystem boundary
5. Ten-screen prototype journey
6. Safety case and AI controls: one serious hazard, prevention, detection, recovery, manual fallback and accepted operating cost
7. Testing and pilot evidence: 79 tests, Release 0.2 conditional go, 20 controlled scenario runs, no new issue observed in four browser interactions
8. Limits and next decision: reviewer sessions are pending; no pilot-driven product change; production, clinical, fairness and security evidence remain open

The presentation must be deliverable in under ten minutes.

## Evidence rules

- Describe product intent without claiming alignment to a named company's mission.
- Segment users by workflow relationship and usage pattern; do not add demographics without a relevant, evidenced product reason.
- Present named competitors only in an optional sourced appendix prepared under `competitive_approach_evidence_plan.md`.
- Keep planned hypotheses, executed findings and achieved outcomes visibly separate.
- State at least one decision retained, changed, deferred or stopped because of recorded evidence; do not invent a lesson before review or testing occurs.
- For the safety segment, distinguish the proposed hazard analysis from executed prototype evidence and unresolved real-world uncertainty.

## Current presentation decision

Use a **deferred** decision as the evidence-backed example: the synthetic pilot found no supported product change, so Section 4 revision work is deferred until structured reviewer feedback exists. Do not present this as validation that no change is needed.
